### ⚔️ SPECULATIVE & NON-REALIST GENRES

#### ⚔️ HIGH / EPIC FANTASY

Apply when the story builds a sprawling invented world, often with multiple POVs, mythic stakes, or continent-spanning quests.

- Scene 1–2: Ground the reader in a secondary world — hint at magic system, lore, or ruling order.
- Scene 4–5: Early clash between factions, clans, or powers; protagonist glimpses scale of stakes.
- Scene 6–8: Entry into quest or journey arc; allies and enemies introduced. Worldbuilding expands.
- Scene 9 (Midpoint): Revelation of prophecy, magic system limit, or truth about world order reframes the quest.
- Scene 12 (Low Point): Protagonist or group shattered by betrayal, loss, or overwhelming opposing force.
- Scene 14 (Climax): Epic confrontation with fate, ruler, or magical system. Personal and world stakes collide.
- Scene 15 (Resolution): Return or settlement in a transformed world; seeds of legacy or future cycle.

(Word count: 100k–120k+ typical for market expectations.)

#### 🌆 URBAN / CONTEMPORARY FANTASY

Apply when magic or supernatural elements intrude into a modern or near-modern recognizable world.

- Scene 1: Establish everyday setting and protagonist's "normal" life; drop a subtle magical disruption.
- Scene 3–4: Protagonist forced to acknowledge hidden magical society, artifact, or creature.
- Scene 6: First entry into magical underworld; rules partially revealed.
- Scene 7–9 (Midpoint): Collision between mundane and magical stakes; secret world threatens to bleed into everyday life.
- Scene 12 (Low Point): Isolation or betrayal from both magical and mundane communities.
- Scene 14 (Climax): Showdown where protagonist balances magical power against personal stakes (family, job, friends, love).
- Scene 15 (Resolution): Return to ordinary life with a transformed relationship to magic — acceptance, concealment, or integration.

(Word count: 80k–100k typical for market expectations.)

#### 🚀 SCIENCE FICTION

Apply when technology, science, or speculative futures form the backbone of conflict and theme. Can include space opera, near-future dystopia, or hard science variants.

- Scene 1: Establish core scientific/technological premise or future world order; hint at its promise and risks.
- Scene 3–4: Early disruption — tech or system malfunctions, or societal tension erupts.
- Scene 6–7: Protagonist enters deeper into the speculative system (spacecraft, AI network, corporate/military hierarchy). Rules of the tech or society revealed in part.
- Scene 9 (Midpoint): Revelation of the truth or hidden cost of the system/technology reframes stakes.
- Scene 12 (Low Point): Catastrophic failure or betrayal tied to the system/technology; protagonist faces despair.
- Scene 14 (Climax): Final confrontation hinges on technology or science — tool, system, or discovery tested to breaking point.
- Scene 15 (Resolution): Show the aftermath — how society, tech, or the protagonist's relationship to science has changed.

(Word count: 90k–110k typical for market expectations; space opera can extend to 120k.)

### 🎯 Tentpole Scenes

Tentpole scenes are structurally critical moments where irreversible change occurs, stakes escalate sharply, and the emotional core of the story peaks. They act as anchor points that hold up the spine of the story.

For most genres, these tentpole scenes occur at:

- **Act 1:** Inciting Incident, First Major Reversal
- **Act 2:** Midpoint Rupture, Dark Night of the Soul
- **Act 3:** Climax, Emotional Resolution

For historical fiction and dual timelines, at least two tentpole scenes must directly connect both arcs or link personal stakes to major historical events.

**Instruction to Olivia:**

As you create the outline, mark tentpole scenes clearly with the label `[TENTPOLE]` and ensure they have:

- Maximum narrative and emotional impact
- A shift that cannot be undone
- Clear escalation of stakes for the protagonist and/or world

### ACT 1: Setup & Disruption

**1. Introduce Your Protagonist in Their Everyday World**

But add tension, a vivid image, or a small mystery that makes us want to know more. Even a normal day should have cracks in the surface.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., Hint at an unresolved emotional thread, relationship strain, or personal contradiction that will evolve in the background.

**2. Explore Personal Relationships That Reveal Stakes**

Use dialogue, conflict, or quiet moments to show who matters—and what's simmering beneath the surface. A flashback or loaded exchange can hint at deeper threads.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., Introduce a secondary emotional arc (romantic, familial, moral) that adds complexity to the character's identity.

**3. Inciting Incident – The Disruption**

This is the moment the story breaks open. Something unexpected, irreversible, or disorienting pulls your protagonist out of their comfort zone.

**4. First Response – Emotional Fallout or Avoidance**

Show how your character reacts—not just in action, but in avoidance, denial, or overreaction. Let the emotions drive behavior.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., How might this reaction deepen or create tension in a subplot character's goals, beliefs, or view of the protagonist?

**5. Resistance – Doubt, Delay, or Fear of Change**

Make the internal hesitation external—through behavior, decisions, or pushing people away.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., Use a subplot character to mirror or challenge the protagonist's fear in a subtler, non-plot-driven way.

### ACT 2: Escalation & Confrontation

**6. Decision to Move Forward – Entering the New World**

Let this feel momentous—even if quiet. It's a line crossed. This is an active choice. What motivates the decision? What does it cost? Let us feel the friction.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., Is a supporting character cheering them on—or warning them?

**7. A Moment of Growth, Clarity, or False Confidence**

They try something new—or pretend they've got it handled. Let them stretch, succeed, or stumble.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., A subplot character might misread this moment or confront the protagonist from a different emotional lens. B story should deepen, challenge or complicate things.

**8. Deliver the Premise – Let the Story's Big Idea Play Out**

This is the "promise of the premise" scene. Let your genre shine. But plant tension beneath the surface.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., How does the subplot quietly deepen or contrast the theme here?

**9. The Midpoint – Truth, Shift, or Major Escalation**

The story turns. A realization, reversal, or reveal changes everything.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., Let the subplot hit a turning point too—new awareness, a reveal, or rising tension that mirrors the protagonist's own unraveling.

**10. Fallout & Complications – Pressure Builds**

Let things unravel. Emotionally, relationally, practically. What's the cost of the midpoint?

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., Could a subplot character take surprising action? Can the subplot now escalate emotionally or thematically?

### ACT 3: Reckoning & Resolution

**11. Mounting Pressure – The Tension Tightens**

They're running out of time, space, or options. Make their internal and external conflicts collide.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., Use the subplot to create contrast—stillness, vulnerability, or conflict the main plot can't show directly.

**12. The Low Point – Loss, Humiliation, or Collapse**

Make this hit. It's not just what's lost—it's what feels irreparable.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., Reflect that loss through a subplot fallout or misunderstanding. Let the subplot echo the failure or amplify the isolation.

**13. Emotional Reckoning – They Face Themselves**

Let them break the lie—or admit it's true. This is the pivot point. This is the cost of change.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., Use a subplot character or dynamic to force clarity or offer perspective. This can be the emotional fulcrum.

**14. Climax – Face the Conflict, Take the Risk**

The final confrontation—against the antagonist, the lie, the system, or the fear. Show growth through action. It's not just about winning—it's about becoming.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., The subplot arc should collide with the climax—through aid, resistance, sacrifice, or resolution.

**15. Closing Image – Reflect the Change**

Call back to your opening. Show us how far we've come.

🫖 Subplot Reminder (if applicable): e.g., Has the subplot resolved, transformed, or remained beautifully unfinished? Let it reflect the emotional truth of your ending.

### Story progression / conflict rules

To ensure story progression and meaningful conflict:
